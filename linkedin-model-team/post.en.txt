The stronger model returns when development is finished.

That is the change I am making in AI-assisted development:

1. A stronger model prepares requirements and the plan.
2. A lower-cost model carries out the full development assignment.
3. Once finished, the stronger model receives the complete deliverable to review and fix.

I do not call the stronger model after every file, tool action, or task. Doing that would mean paying for its involvement throughout the workflow.

While the lower-cost model develops, the harness delivers tasks, retains state, and supplies context and tools. The stronger model waits for the final handoff.

But the approach needs preparation: documented architecture, working instructions—skills—concrete requirements, and tests that actually run. A good environment can help the lower-cost model deliver something closer to the requirements than a stronger model working from an ambiguous request.

One example from the note: email validation.

→ The lower-cost model completes the tests and implementation for R1 and R2.
→ The stronger model reviews the deliverable and checks whether “ana@” is accepted when it should be rejected.
→ If there is a fault, the stronger model fixes it and the result is verified.

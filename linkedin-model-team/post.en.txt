A cheap call can turn into an expensive task.

I am changing how I develop with AI: a lower-cost model implements and fixes; a stronger one prepares the work and reviews the result.

Savings start before I call the model.

First, I equip the developer with the project architecture, working instructions—skills—tools, clear requirements, and tests. A harness then controls what context it receives, what it can execute, and what evidence it returns.

A harness is the program that makes this workflow run. A well-equipped lower-cost model can deliver something closer to the requirements than a stronger model working from an ambiguous request. That needs checking on real tasks, not judging by the model name.

One example from the note: email validation.

→ The developer receives R1 and R2: reject “ana@” and allow “ana@example.com”.
→ The reviewer compares changes with those requirements and looks for concrete failures.
→ Findings return to the developer; the correction is then reviewed and checks are run.

In the guide, I explain how I am building this with Chalc:

• How I equip the developer to request verifiable code.
• What the orchestrator does and what each agent receives.
• How I review code, tests, and reports.
• Which benefits I seek and when corrections consume the savings.
• How I distinguish using fewer tokens from paying less for them.

My measure is the full cost per accepted task, including failed attempts and my review time.
